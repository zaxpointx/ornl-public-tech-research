## The eight directorates: a starting map, not an organization chart

ORNL's public overview describes eight science directorates [KNOWN: S01]. Descriptions below report their stated areas, not exclusive ownership of every example. Organization names differ slightly between ORNL's directorates and leadership pages; check those pages before relying on current names [KNOWN: S01, S02].

1. Biological and Environmental Systems Science: biology, ecology, data and Earth systems [KNOWN: S01]. In the SPRUCE warming experiment, a published study reported increased shrub-layer biomass with warming over eight years; its result concerns the studied peatland, not a prediction for every ecosystem [KNOWN: S04]. The project reports that its whole-ecosystem and deep-peat warming treatments operated through December 31, 2025; a 2026 update supersedes an earlier environmental dataset and corrects earlier values [KNOWN: E01]. The project's December 2025 status also said phased decommissioning would begin in 2026; its completion and current monitoring status are unknown [KNOWN: S03]. The example illustrates the broader science; a specific directorate reporting line is [UNKNOWN].

   For a safe public-data starting point, the [SPRUCE environmental dataset record](https://mnspruce.ornl.gov/datasets/spruce-whole-ecosystems-warming-wew-environmental-data-marcell-experimental-forest) describes half-hourly environmental observations from 2014–2025, version 2.0 [KNOWN: E01]. The data files were not downloaded or independently validated; check its repository for current access and reuse conditions.
2. Energy Science and Technology: research spanning energy generation, distribution, storage and end use [KNOWN: S01]. A 2023 report from the directorate modeled widespread geothermal heat-pump adoption and home weatherization: in its base scenario, required annual electricity generation in the contiguous United States in 2050 was 585 TWh lower than the no-deployment scenario [KNOWN: D01]. This is a modeled scenario, not realized savings or a prediction for a particular building. For manufacturing, read [the existing additive-manufacturing study](../deep-dives/additive-manufacturing.md) and the cross-facility story below; exact organizational ownership of each tool remains separately evidenced [UNKNOWN].
3. Fusion and Fission Energy Science: research on fission and fusion systems, advanced reactors and existing fleet challenges [KNOWN: S01]. In a 2020 DIII-D tokamak study, authors reported that penetration of injected shattered-pellet fragments decreased as plasma energy increased; they also cautioned that extrapolating to ITER needs validated modeling [KNOWN: D02]. This is a reported experimental result relevant to fusion challenges, not proof of a solution at ITER; precise directorate ownership of the publication is [UNKNOWN]. The existing [reactor software study](../deep-dives/nuclear-reactor-software.md) links public tool briefs; it is not a complete fusion or fission survey.
4. Isotope Science and Enrichment: isotope research, production and enrichment [KNOWN: S01]; see the HFIR and hot-cell example below. The name on ORNL's leadership page is Isotope Science and Engineering [KNOWN: S02], so neither label is silently substituted for the other.
5. Physical Sciences: materials, chemistry, nanoscience and physics, among other fields [KNOWN: S01]. The autonomous scanning-probe microscopy example below is one public research result, not a comprehensive directorate portfolio [KNOWN: S08].
6. National Security: ORNL describes research addressing cyber-physical threats and other security challenges [KNOWN: S01]. Its research-output directory lists [Cyberwheel](../deep-dives/cyberwheel/synthesis.md) under Cybersecurity Research, whose page is associated with the National Security Sciences Directorate [KNOWN: Z01]. This is a directory association as fetched, not proof of the project's administrative owner or present team. The 2024 Cyberwheel paper reports initial agent results in simulation, not a live network [KNOWN: S09].
7. Neutron Sciences: neutron scattering research using SNS and HFIR [KNOWN: S15]. In a 2024 paper, an international team used the BASIS spectrometer at SNS to study the motion of a model protein in separating liquid phases, and proposed an interpretation of its short-lived clusters [KNOWN: N01]. This is a model-system hypothesis, not a medical finding. The [existing SNS data pipeline](../deep-dives/sns-neutron-pipeline.md) shows selected published software, not every facility workflow; a separately documented NOMAD data-challenge dataset illustrates one public data record [KNOWN: N03].
8. Computing and Computational Sciences: computational research and the Oak Ridge Leadership Computing Facility (OLCF) [KNOWN: S01]. In 2024, a University of Melbourne-led team including ORNL computing researcher Dmytro Bykov won a Gordon Bell Prize for Frontier quantum molecular dynamics work [KNOWN: D03]. ORNL's facility article reports a simulated system of over two million correlated electrons [KNOWN: D04]; this is a computation, not an experimental measurement or a proven drug. See the existing [test harness study](../deep-dives/olcf-test-harness.md) and the cross-facility example below.

## Connected story 1: can a print learn from measurement?

A metal part may retain internal stress after it cools. INTERSECT's autonomous additive manufacturing example is an attempt to connect observations, simulation and decisions across the Manufacturing Demonstration Facility (MDF), SNS and OLCF [KNOWN: S05, S06]. ORNL reports that the architecture specification was released as version 1.0 and says the research workflow was deployed across the three facilities; this is a reported research deployment, not proof of routine industrial operation or completed qualification [KNOWN: S05; UNKNOWN: independent outcome metrics].

The documented design has two different feedback loops. In one, temperature observations feed a simulation and the simulation informs settings for the next printed layer. In the other, neutron diffraction and imaging help validate the simulation and inform the next part [KNOWN: S07]. These are descriptions of a design pattern: they do not prove that every future build uses every step. The architecture sets a target of residual stress at least twice as close to the desired distribution; it does not present that target as a measured result [KNOWN: S06].

A related but distinct research result is OpeN-AM: ORNL reports wire-arc printing of a steel specimen measured using SNS's VULCAN instrument, with infrared imaging, in an experiment using a platform designed and built at MDF [KNOWN: S11]. This is direct evidence of a manufacturing-to-neutron experiment, not evidence that this experiment was the same INTERSECT feedback-loop deployment [UNKNOWN: S05, S11]. A 2025 paper documents [adamantine](../projects/adamantine.md) and [Flowcept](../projects/flowcept.md) in a related INTERSECT additive-manufacturing software demonstration, but its printer and sensing service were simulated [KNOWN: H02]. Their use in the specific three-facility loop above remains [UNKNOWN: H02, S05–S07]. [Myna](../projects/myna.md) and [3DThesis](../projects/3dthesis.md) remain relevant context, not established components of that loop [UNKNOWN: S05–S07, S12].

The [longer connected story](intersect-additive-story.md) separates the architecture's goals, the published neutron experiment and the simulated-printer software demonstration.

**A research result across tools and labs:** How might heating during a metal 3D print affect its microscopic grain structure? In a 2025 study, researchers used ORNL's Myna to set up 54 3DThesis thermal simulations, 78 AdditiveFOAM melt-pool simulations and three ExaCA microstructure simulations from a public MDF build dataset. They compared predictions for three part regions with measurements. The models predicted differences in grain structure, but the measured samples showed little variation across the tested conditions [KNOWN: Y01]. The authors suggest a two-phase model as a possible explanation, not a proven fix. The work demonstrates a research workflow and an important mismatch, not a qualified part or reliable prediction for every alloy.

In a separate study, ORNL's [Finch](../projects/finch.md) heat model was coupled in memory to the LLNL-hosted [ExaCA](https://github.com/LLNL/ExaCA) grain model. The paper reports example simulations and credits resources at both Oak Ridge and Argonne leadership computing facilities [KNOWN: Y02]. We do not know which specific runs used which machine, whether the example was independently validated, or whether it was part of the INTERSECT workflow above [UNKNOWN]. The 2025 Myna study does not establish Finch or adamantine as components of its particular experiment [UNKNOWN: Y01].

## Connected story 2: from neutron measurements to usable data

The public [workflow-manager repository](https://github.com/neutrons/data_workflow) describes a workflow manager and reporting application; the [post-processing agent](https://github.com/neutrons/post_processing_agent) describes automated reduction and cataloging [KNOWN: S13, S14]. In plain language: experimental measurements need to be organized and converted into forms researchers can analyze. The existing [pipeline walkthrough](../deep-dives/sns-neutron-pipeline.md) explains the published components, with [Mantid](../projects/mantid.md), [ONCat](../projects/oncat.md), [SNAPRed](../projects/snapred.md) and [PyRS](../projects/pyrs.md) as linked reading.

A 2020 paper abstract reports moderate workflow speedups from metadata changes and proposes a more disruptive input/output approach; proposal and demonstrated improvement are not the same thing [KNOWN: S16]. The uploader of certain live plots and whether all instruments use the same path remain [UNKNOWN: existing walkthrough]. Do not treat a diagram of published code as a map of a live facility.

## Connected story 3: Cyberwheel is a research environment

Cyberwheel provides simulation and emulation for experiments on autonomous defense agents; its 2024 paper describes initial simulated cyber-deception results [KNOWN: S09]. An ORNL software record separately identifies the published research software and credits authors [KNOWN: S10]. Neither establishes deployment to defend ORNL's real network. Its [existing synthesis](../deep-dives/cyberwheel/synthesis.md) and [claim boundary](../deep-dives/cyberwheel/claim-boundary.md) preserve distinctions between simulation, emulation, and claims about deployment. No claim is made here about Cayden's personal experience.

**A connection across laboratories:** Cyberwheel's v2.0 release says it supports emulation using FIREWHEEL, a tool developed by Sandia National Laboratories [KNOWN: X01, X02]. In simple terms, ORNL's software can evaluate simulated defenders in an emulated environment using a Sandia-developed tool. This is a documented software connection, not evidence that the labs share one team or that the tools protect a real laboratory network. Current compatibility and the result of any particular local run remain [UNKNOWN].

An ORNL research directory now provides a narrower organizational connection than the earlier brief could find: it lists Cyberwheel as a Cybersecurity Research output and associates that directory page with the National Security Sciences Directorate [KNOWN: Z01]. The administrative owner remains [UNKNOWN]. A separate 2023 AI ATAC paper evaluated eight malware-detection tools against a research file set; ORNL associates that paper with multiple organizations, including National Security Sciences [KNOWN: Z02]. The two outputs share a directory listing, not an established common system or deployment [INFERRED: Z01, Z02].

## Connected story 4: autonomy still needs scientists

ORNL reports a scanning-probe microscopy proof of concept in which an algorithm selected measurements using learned information and physical reasoning; in a studied ferroelectric material, the reported experiment found relevant defects [KNOWN: S08]. Separately, an INTERSECT demonstration passed sample information from a digital microscope twin to a real computer and back, repeating a measurement-selection cycle; it was a demonstration, not a claim that all microscopes operate this way [KNOWN: S17]. The two examples are related in concept [INFERRED: S08, S17], not established here as the same implementation [UNKNOWN].

The longer [autonomous science story](autonomous-science.md) follows the published ferroelectric result, a microscope-software demonstration and the distinct AutoFlowS chemistry project, separating findings from goals and noting shared authorship without assuming shared software.

## Connected story 5: a reactor, isotopes and shielded processing

ORNL's April 2026 Radioisotope Development Laboratory factsheet describes targets fabricated at that laboratory, irradiated at HFIR, then returned for processing and purification of actinium-227; it says production began in 2017 [KNOWN: S18]. This is a public, high-level description of a particular isotope pathway, not a procedure or a description of site layout. ORNL separately describes a future Radioisotope Processing Facility planned to be operational by 2039 [KNOWN: S19]. A planned date is not an operating facility, and present output or schedule changes are [UNKNOWN].

The longer [isotopes and hot cells story](isotopes-and-hot-cells.md) follows that medical supply pathway, a californium research study linking measured results to reactor software, and NASA's plutonium-238 power-system supply chain. It keeps reported operations, modeled comparisons, historical demonstrations and future targets separate.
