# Book plan

## Working title

**The Public Technology of Oak Ridge: An Evidence-Bounded Atlas of Software, Data, and Research Infrastructure**

## Editorial thesis

ORNL's public technology is best understood as a connected portfolio of research artifacts rather than a single product stack. This book reconstructs that portfolio from public evidence, showing what each artifact does, how projects relate, how maturity changes across projects, and where the public record stops.

## Intended readers

- Researchers and students entering scientific software or laboratory technology.
- Engineers evaluating public research software before adoption.
- Research-software professionals studying governance, provenance, and sustainability.
- Curious technical readers who need plain-English explanations with evidence boundaries.

## Canonical chapter map

### Part I — How to read the evidence

1. **Scope and limits** — what public-source research can and cannot establish.
2. **How the atlas was built** — cataloging, evidence labels, two-sweep verification, and negative findings.
3. **How a national laboratory appears from the outside** — the lab tree, public organizations, repositories, records, and project signals.

### Part II — The portfolio

4. **Computing foundations** — HPC, runtimes, solvers, benchmarks, and numerical infrastructure.
5. **Data and scientific workflows** — provenance, federation, live data, visualization, and post-processing.
6. **Energy, nuclear, and fusion** — simulation, thermodynamics, reactor analysis, and plasma-facing materials.
7. **Neutrons and experimental science** — instrument data, reduction pipelines, and facility-facing software.
8. **AI, quantum, and cyber** — graph learning, quantum toolchains, and autonomous defense research.
9. **Materials, climate, buildings, and transport** — public tools that connect simulation to real-world systems.

### Part III — Case studies

10. **Cyberwheel** — reinforcement-learning cyber defense as a research test bed.
11. **The SNS neutron data pipeline** — from instrument output to analyzed result.
12. **The OLCF test harness** — automated acceptance testing for supercomputers.
13. **Additive manufacturing** — the next deep dive, once its timing and connections work is complete.

### Part IV — Connections and conclusions

14. **What is actually connected** — dependency, co-use, institutional, and evidence relationships.
15. **Maturity without overclaiming** — studied, designed, tested, released, integrated, and deployed are not interchangeable.
16. **What the public record leaves unknown** — missing artifacts, ambiguous ownership, and responsible stopping boundaries.
17. **A field guide for reading public research technology** — transferable lessons and future work.

## Chapter contract

Every finished chapter or case study must have a thesis, defined scope, source-backed claims, explicit evidence labels, a list of unknowns, a maturity statement, and links back to the catalog or source ledger. Use [the chapter checklist](../templates/chapter-checklist.md) before marking a chapter complete.

## Completion definition

The book is ready for a numbered edition when every chapter in the map is either complete or explicitly labeled as a bounded gap; catalog counts are generated and pass validation; load-bearing sources have retrieval dates and locators; the verification sweep is current; and the publication checklist passes.

## Current state

The repository currently has a complete atlas foundation, 48 briefs, 3 completed deep dives, and a substantial connections and verification layer. Parts I and II are underway; Part III is partial; Part IV is the primary synthesis gap.
